## About Me

I'm a healthcare data analyst and Data Science student at Oregon State University. At work, I build and maintain production data pipelines in BigQuery and Dataform, and I built the insights data model my organization uses to make decisions. That work showed me how much depends on clean, reliable data, and it's what pulled me toward data science. I want to go beyond preparing data to modeling it, using predictive and inferential methods to answer real questions directly.

Before working in data, I served in the Peace Corps. My counterparts there didn't speak English, so I built programs in Thai. That taught me to communicate across differences and to lead without formal authority, and I still rely on both when I work across teams.

At OSU, I'm pursuing the Advanced Data Science option to build a stronger foundation in statistics, mathematics and machine learning. Outside of work and school, I'm a new dad, which keeps me focused on using my time well. I'm especially interested in healthcare, where good models can directly improve patient outcomes.

## Where I'm Headed

My near-term goal is to move from data analysis into a data scientist role. I work with production data systems every day, building pipelines in BigQuery and Dataform, but most of my work ends once the data is ready for someone else to use. I want to be the one who builds the predictive and inferential models that answer the question directly, and who can explain and defend the results.

My job posting analysis earlier in this course made this goal more concrete. Both postings I compared asked for strong SQL, Python, and statistical modeling, along with the ability to communicate findings to people who aren't technical. SQL is already one of my strengths from work, but the analysis showed me that Python and applied machine learning are where I need to grow. It also showed me that employers expect evidence of those skills, not just a degree.

To close that gap, I'm working on three things. The first is my OSU coursework in statistics, mathematics, and machine learning, which gives me the theory behind the tools. The second is building public projects, starting with a gradient boosting model in BigQuery ML, so my skills are visible beyond my job. The third is learning to validate models and test them for bias. My skills self-reflection pointed to this as an area I want to strengthen, and it matters in healthcare because a model's errors affect real patients.

Longer term, I want to move from task-level work to system-level work, designing machine learning systems rather than individual models. I'm open to applying that in healthcare, pharma, or finance. After graduating, I plan to pursue a master's degree, because I want my knowledge to genuinely match my credentials.
